Quantified temporal risk partitioning among tigers, five focal prey species, and human activity using camera-trap data from 149 stations (12,835 trap-nights) across Royal Manas National Park, Phibsoo Wildlife Sanctuary, and Sarpang Forest Division. Tiger–prey temporal overlap varied substantially by species (Δ4 range 0.671–0.909), with wild boar showing the highest exploratory temporal-exposure score and gaur the lowest. Tiger–human temporal separation was pronounced (Δ4 = 0.397), and tiger nocturnality showed a positive but statistically uncertain association with local human-use rates (beta regression, n = 13 stations). Conditional diel analysis suggested dhole showed the strongest apparent activity shift at tiger-present stations while ungulates remained stable. Results indicate temporal exposure in tiger landscapes is species-specific and multi-dimensional, providing a behavioural reference condition for monitoring future anthropogenic encroachment in a relatively undisturbed Himalayan landscape.
