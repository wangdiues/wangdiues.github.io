Developed and validated a paired-model SDM framework that separates fundamental environmental suitability from realized habitat under anthropogenic constraints, applied to Asian elephants across a 2,607 km² gradient of protected forest and agricultural frontier in south-central Bhutan (Royal Manas National Park, Phibsoo Wildlife Sanctuary, Biological Corridor 03, and adjoining farmland). Using 1,049 elephant occurrence records and 65 verified human–elephant conflict locations, the Full Model incorporating anthropogenic predictors substantially outperformed the environment-only Ideal Model (AUC 0.896 vs. 0.848). Anthropogenic pressure reduced highly suitable habitat by ~57 km² (~10%) while degrading a further 410 km², and documented conflict clustered in high-quality core habitat (mean suitability 0.733 vs. 0.197 landscape-wide) — demonstrating that human–elephant conflict reflects niche overlap in productive landscapes rather than displacement into marginal areas. The framework yields a three-category conservation triage map — restoration targets, mitigation priorities, and deprioritized areas — transferable to any wide-ranging species where occurrence data and anthropogenic predictor layers exist.
