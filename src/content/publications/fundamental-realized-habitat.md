Developed and validated a paired-model SDM framework that separates fundamental environmental suitability from realized habitat under anthropogenic constraints, applied to Asian elephants across the protected forest–agricultural frontier of south-central Bhutan (Royal Manas National Park, Phibsoo Wildlife Sanctuary, Biological Corridor 03 and adjoining farmland). Using 1,049 elephant occurrence records and 65 verified human–elephant conflict locations at ~842 m resolution, the Full Model incorporating anthropogenic predictors outperformed the environment-only Ideal Model (AUC 0.896 vs. 0.848) and reduced suitable habitat from 555 km² to 498 km² — an exclusion of roughly 57 km² of high-quality habitat by human pressure. Conflict locations clustered within high-suitability core habitat rather than marginal zones, indicating that human–elephant conflict reflects niche overlap in shared high-quality landscapes rather than displacement into poor habitat.
