Documented 101 wild edible and medicinal plant species across Yagang and Gengu villages, Darla Gewog, Chhukha (48 medicinal-only, 38 edible-only, 15 dual-use), drawing on semi-structured interviews and field-based plant-use records yielding 58 valid respondent records and 101 plant records. Household reliance on WEMPs was nearly universal (98.3%), primarily for medicine (96.5%) and food (80.7%), with minimal income contribution (1.8%). Knowledge transmission ran overwhelmingly through grandparents (98.2%), indicating strong intergenerational continuity, and gendered roles were pronounced — men more frequently involved in collection, women primarily responsible for preparation and storage. Most respondents (94.1%) perceived declining WEMP populations, attributing this to cattle grazing, unsustainable harvesting, and land-use change, yet expressed strong support for domestication and integration into agroforestry systems. Findings are perception-based and specific to the two villages; they argue for localised, gender-responsive, agroforestry-based strategies sustaining WEMP systems within Bhutan's NWFP governance framework.
