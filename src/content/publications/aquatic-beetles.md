Assessed the diversity of predaceous diving beetles (Dytiscidae) in relation to physico-chemical water parameters using stratified random sampling across lentic and lotic water bodies at three gewogs in Dagana, Bhutan. From 664 individuals collected, five subfamilies, 12 genera, and 17 species were identified. Species diversity and taxon richness declined with altitude and showed moderately negative correlation with pH, while temperature correlated positively and emerged as the effective determinant of Dytiscid distribution in canonical correspondence analysis; diversity peaked in marshy habitat (H′ = 2.36) and the 800–1,200 m stratum (H′ = 2.07). The results identify priority freshwater habitats for protection before degradation, contributing baseline data for aquatic insect conservation in the Himalayas.
