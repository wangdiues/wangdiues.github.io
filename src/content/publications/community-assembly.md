Analysed camera-trap data from 149 stations (12,835 trap-nights) across Royal Manas National Park, Phibsoo Wildlife Sanctuary, and Sarpang Forest Division to quantify the relative roles of climatic gradients, anthropogenic disturbance, and interspecific interactions in mammalian community assembly along a 64–2,552 m elevational gradient. Non-metric multidimensional scaling identified annual precipitation (BIO12; r² = 0.366) and mean annual temperature (BIO1; r² = 0.311) as the dominant filters on community composition, with distance-to-roads a weaker but significant secondary effect. Of 990 tested species pairs, 214 showed significant co-occurrence structure persisting after controlling for shared habitat preferences, indicating residual biotic organisation. Highland assemblages (yellow-throated marten, marbled cat) separated from lowland assemblages dominated by Asian elephant and tiger, with no evidence of mesopredator release. Seventy-three species recorded; accumulation curves approached asymptote at full sampling effort.
