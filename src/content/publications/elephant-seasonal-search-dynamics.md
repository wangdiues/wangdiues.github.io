Evaluated seasonal movement-state allocation, movement clustering, vegetation greenness, and seasonal space use from GPS telemetry of four collared Asian elephants in the Gelephu–Sarpang foothills. A three-state hidden Markov model classified 39,566 steps into resting-like (24.2%), foraging-like (27.7%), and travelling-like (48.0%) states with substantial individual variation. First-passage time analysis identified movement clustering at 4,183–5,000 m radii and seven candidate area-restricted-search zones, while MODIS NDVI phenology confirmed strong seasonal greenness variation (mean amplitude = 0.786). Seasonal kernel density estimates showed dry-season expansion of 95% ranges — most strongly for one individual (347.8 km² dry season vs. 123.7 km² monsoon) — and exploratory anomaly screening flagged 9.4% of fixes as statistical outliers for field validation. Inference is restricted to the four tracked individuals.
