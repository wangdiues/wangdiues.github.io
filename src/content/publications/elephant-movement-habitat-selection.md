Applied a movement-aware analytical framework — autocorrelated kernel density estimation, integrated step selection functions, and hidden Markov models — to GPS satellite telemetry from four collared Asian elephants in the Gelephu–Sarpang region of south-central Bhutan (39,578 validated fixes at 10-minute intervals over 328–545 days per individual). AKDE 95% home ranges varied twelvefold among individuals (170–2,036 km²), reflecting divergent individual movement tactics. Habitat selection showed strong avoidance of high-elevation terrain (hazard ratio = 0.26) and cropland (HR = 0.90) with positive selection for vegetation greenness (NDVI; HR = 1.15), while a conventional global RSF returned null effects attributable to spatial autocorrelation — demonstrating that movement-aware modelling recovers biologically coherent selection signals that IID-based frameworks obscure. Three behavioural states were resolved (Resting 16–30%, Foraging 23–40%, Traveling 37–62% of fixes). Inference is restricted to the four tracked individuals.
